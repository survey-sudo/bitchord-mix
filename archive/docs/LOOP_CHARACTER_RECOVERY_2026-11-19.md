## Product claim

Recover LoopFix's loop-oriented character: prefer a locally usable, safely
executable instrumental loop over a blend when a valid loop opportunity exists.
Phrase analysis may improve a loop's placement, but uncertain structural labels
or broad whole-track preferences must not silently redefine physical feasibility.

This is not an always-loop policy. Local vocal/lyric protection, supported timing,
source identity, complete PCM coverage, finite-loop completion, cancellation and
safe handover remain requirements. Their precise precedence is being reconciled
by the council before implementation.

The reported Adrenaline (ATEEZ) -> Bloody Paradise (ENHYPEN) transition is a
regression case, never a song whitelist. General policy restoration and proving
that exact transition restored are distinct claims.

The user also reports many current mixes labelled Echo or Filter. Treat this as
a broader routing-concentration observation, not a quantified vc83 result. Check
whether usable loops/blends are rejected, unavailable because evidence is
missing, or accepted and then lost during preparation. Count selected and
executed techniques separately on fixed inputs. More variety is not itself a
quality metric: no technique quotas, forced loops or random rotation.

## Neural and NPU direction

Keep analysis -> discrete cues -> bounded preference -> deterministic rendering
as the boundary. Improve musical evidence and candidate ranking alongside the
restored baseline; do not let a ranker repair a candidate that earlier rules
already discarded incorrectly.

BitChord already uses learned beat/downbeat and vocal analysis. Richer local
phrase, pickup and loop-suitability evidence is the useful next quality target;
NPU acceleration is a separately measured means of making that work affordable.

The staged `nirovi_qnn` candidate is a different model. Its failed agreement
with the incumbent is not a ground-truth accuracy verdict. Both enable flags
remain off; exact weight rights and actual BitChord HTP behavior are unresolved.
